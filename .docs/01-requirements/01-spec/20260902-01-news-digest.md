## 1. Problem & Users

Actors affected: general news readers in Thailand (including MFU-area students) who currently follow Thai news by visiting multiple news websites directly in a browser. The team's original hypothesis (drafted 2026-09-02, before any outside data existed) has now been checked against a 7-respondent survey. The picture that comes back is real but moderate — not the acute, unanimous pain the original hypothesis implied — and it remains survey-only evidence: the course gate's required ≥5 live interviews have not yet happened (4 of the 7 respondents opted in as leads for that round, see Q10). Treat P1–P4 as survey-supported, interview-pending, not interview-validated.

- P1: "Occasionally falls behind on the news — moderate, not chronic. Feels like they're missing important news 'Sometimes' (5/7 respondents; 1/7 'Never', 0/7 'Often/Always'); typically finds out about a major news event the same day (2/7) or the next day (3/7) rather than being up to date (1/7)." — Source: NaraNews News Habits & Preferences Survey (Google Form), n=7, Q2+Q3, 2026-09-02–2026-09-06. Revises the original 2026-09-02 hypothesis wording ("Falling behind on the news") down from acute to moderate, per this data.
- P2: "Scattered news sources are a real, consistent annoyance. Most respondents check 3+ different sites/apps (3-5 sites: 3/7, 5+ sites: 1/7; only 3/7 check just 1-2); every one of the 7 respondents rated the annoyance of checking multiple sites at 3-out-of-5 or higher (mean 3.4/5, no respondent below 3)." — Source: NaraNews survey, n=7, Q4+Q5, 2026-09-02–2026-09-06. The best- and most consistently supported of the three original pains.
- P3: "Getting through the news takes more effort than readers want to spend — but this is the most mixed of the three pains. Most already keep daily reading short (5-15 min/day: 4/7, <5 min: 2/7, 15-30 min: 1/7, none 30+/day); opinion is split on whether a one-line summary would be enough (Yes 3/7, Depends 3/7, No 1/7); interest in a summarizing Chrome extension is positive but not unanimous (Yes 4/7, Maybe 2/7, No 1/7)." — Source: NaraNews survey, n=7, Q6+Q7+Q8, 2026-09-02–2026-09-06. Weaker/more mixed support than originally hypothesized — revised down from an unqualified "takes too much time" framing.
- P4: "No dedicated way to be notified when Chrome is open but idle, other than the existing email fallback — readers are evenly split on their preferred idle-state notification channel: Chrome system notification 3/7, Email digest 3/7, don't need to be notified 1/7." — Source: NaraNews survey, n=7, Q9, 2026-09-02–2026-09-06. New pain surfaced by this survey round (not present in the original 2026-09-02 hypothesis); this is the open gap CLAUDE.md/00-proposal.md/02-design flagged (a "Chrome system notification" delivery state with no F# yet).

## 2. Functional Requirements

### F1 — Aggregate headlines from multiple Thai news websites [Must]
As a Thai news reader, I want NaraNews to fetch headlines from multiple Thai news websites into one feed, so that I don't have to visit scattered individual news sites to see what's happening.
Traces to: P2

### F2 — One-line summarized, clickable scrollable headline list [Must]
As a Thai news reader, I want each fetched headline shown as a one-line summary in a clickable, scrollable popup list — where clicking an item jumps me to the full source article — so that I can scan the latest news quickly and only spend time reading the full article when a headline actually interests me.
Traces to: P3

### F3 — Email fallback when not active in Chrome [Must]
As a Thai news reader, I want to receive a news digest by email when I'm not currently browsing in Chrome, so that I still stay up to date with the latest news even when I'm away from the browser.
Traces to: P1

### F4 — Follow-up on specific breaking news [Won't]
As a Thai news reader, I want NaraNews to follow up with me specifically when a story I'm tracking becomes breaking news, so that I don't miss major developments on topics I care about.
Traces to: P1

Note: the product owner described this feature in their own words as a "future plan, optional" item — exploratory, not committed for this DISCOVER-phase spec. Per product-owner direction, it is recorded as Won't-have this phase (see Scope) rather than Should/Could.

### F5 — Chrome system notification delivery when Chrome is idle [Should]
As a Thai news reader who has Chrome open but is not actively browsing (idle), I want to receive a Chrome system notification with the digest, so that I find out about new headlines without needing an active popup check and without relying solely on the email fallback, which I may not want.
Traces to: P4

Note on priority: Q9 survey data is genuinely tied (Chrome system notification 3/7 vs. Email digest 3/7, with 1/7 wanting neither), and is survey-only — not yet confirmed by any of the ≥5 required live interviews. Per this agent's rule to default away from Must when priority isn't clearly obvious from the evidence, F5 is assigned Should (in scope this phase, but intentionally excluded from the Must-only core workflow) rather than Must. This is a judgment call, not a live-interview-confirmed priority; the AskUserQuestion tool was not available in this run to confirm it interactively — flagged for the calling session/product owner to override if they want Must instead.

## 3. Non-Functional Requirements

No non-functional requirements are specified in this phase. Plausible candidates exist for F1–F3 (e.g., a maximum feed-refresh interval for P1/"occasionally falls behind on the news," a minimum number of distinct sources aggregated for P2/"scattered news sources," or a maximum email-fallback dispatch latency), but none of the raw notes, survey data, or product-owner answers this run supplied a concrete number, count, or percentage target, and per rule this agent will not invent one. These should be raised with the product owner as a follow-up (see end-of-run report) and added as NFR1+ once concrete targets are confirmed.

## 4. Legal Requirements

### LR1 — Purpose-limited collection and use of email address for fallback delivery
Before an email-address field is added to any schema, the system must record a written purpose and lawful basis for storing it (e.g., "used solely to deliver a missed-news digest when the user is not active in Chrome"). The email address may be used only for that stated purpose and must never be repurposed for marketing or any other use without separate, opt-in consent.
Section: PDPA
Traces to: P1, F3

### LR2 — Traffic/access logging for the news-delivery backend
Because NaraNews's backend (the service that fetches headlines and dispatches the email fallback, and — if F5 is built — triggers the Chrome-notification check) is a "service provider" under Thai law, every HTTP entry point it exposes must log timestamp (UTC), source IP, route/method, status code, bytes, user_id/session_id (if authenticated), and user-agent for each request, and retain these logs for at least 90 days.
Section: CCA §26
Traces to: F1, F3, F5

### LR3 — Verified identity required before email dispatch
Because F3 sends email to a specific address tied to a specific user, the system must be able to map the session/token used to trigger that dispatch to a verified real identity (email verified at sign-up); the email-fallback channel must not be enabled from an unverified or anonymous session.
Section: CCA §26
Traces to: F3

### LR4 — Recorded consent to Terms of Service / Privacy Policy for email fallback
Because enabling F3 requires collecting and storing an email address, when the user clicks "I agree" to the Terms of Service or Privacy Policy in order to turn on the email-fallback feature, the system must record user_id, authenticated session, document_version_hash, timestamp, IP, and the exact button text clicked. A bare `accepted=true` flag is not sufficient evidence of consent/intent.
Section: ETA §9/26/28
Traces to: F3

### LR5 — Purpose-limited storage of delivery-channel preference
Before adding a "notification channel preference" field (Chrome system notification vs. email vs. none) needed to route a reader to their chosen idle-state channel for F5, the system must record a written purpose and lawful basis for that field in the schema/PR comment (e.g., "used solely to route the missed-news digest to the reader's chosen idle-state channel"); the preference must not be reused for any other purpose (e.g. marketing, analytics) without separate, opt-in consent.
Section: PDPA
Traces to: P4, F5

## 5. Scope

### In scope (this phase)
- F1 — Aggregate headlines from multiple Thai news websites [Must]
- F2 — One-line summarized, clickable scrollable headline list [Must]
- F3 — Email fallback when not active in Chrome [Must]
- F5 — Chrome system notification delivery when Chrome is idle [Should]

### Out of scope (Won't-have)
- F4 — Follow-up on specific breaking news — product owner described this as a "future plan, optional" idea, not planned for this DISCOVER-phase spec; revisit in a later phase.

### Core workflow (built end-to-end this phase)
News Digest Delivery: (1) The backend periodically fetches headlines from multiple Thai news websites (F1). (2) Each fetched headline is summarized into a one-line entry and shown as a clickable, scrollable list in the Chrome popup while the user is actively browsing in Chrome; clicking an entry jumps to the full source article (F2). (3) If the user is not currently active in Chrome, the same digest is instead delivered by email so the user still receives it (F3).
Built entirely from: F1, F2, F3 (all Must). F5 (Should) is in scope this phase but intentionally not part of this core workflow — only Must-priority requirements form the core workflow.
